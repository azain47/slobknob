⏺ I don't know of any app that does this out of the box. Radial menus exist on
  macOS, but none I know of let you pick a slice by turning a knob.

  Closest existing apps:
  - Kando: free, open source pie menu for macOS, Windows and Linux. A shortcut
    opens it, and you pick a slice with the mouse. It doesn't take input from
    the knob.
  - Loop: a radial menu for arranging windows only, not general actions.

  Why knob support is missing: the F75 knob probably sends ordinary media keys:
  Volume Up, Volume Down and Mute. Pie menu apps don't listen for those keys,
  and macOS takes them for its own volume control.

  What would work: Hammerspoon (free Lua scripting app for macOS). It can catch
  media keys and stop macOS from seeing them, and it can draw an overlay on
  screen. The logic would be:

  1. Knob pressed: start a timer of about 300ms.
  2. Released before the timer ends: treat it as a normal tap and toggle mute as
     usual.
  3. Still held when the timer ends: show the wheel overlay in the middle of the
     screen.
  4. Knob turned while the wheel is open: Volume Up and Down move the highlight
     clockwise or counter-clockwise instead of changing the volume.
  5. Knob released: run the highlighted option, such as opening an app,
     controlling media, running a shortcut or a shell command.

  When the wheel isn't open, turning the knob changes the volume as normal.

  Thing to check first: the knob press has to send separate "pressed" and
  "released" signals, or hold can't be detected. Most keyboards do, but I
  haven't confirmed it for the F75. Hammerspoon's console can show the raw key
  events so you can check.

  I can write the Hammerspoon script for you, about 150 lines including the
  wheel drawing. Tell me which options you want on the wheel (for example

❯ is it a good idea ?                                                           

⏺ Yes, if the EventViewer test passes and you have about 6–8 actions you use
  daily. It's cheap to try: free tools and about an hour of setup.

  Why it works:
  - The knob suits a wheel. Each click of the knob moves exactly one slice, so
    you can pick without looking, much like the GTA wheel on a controller.
  - It's one-handed. Your other hand stays on the mouse.
  - You don't memorize shortcuts. The wheel shows the options, so 8 actions cost
    nothing to learn.
  - You lose nothing. A quick tap still mutes and turning still changes volume.
    The wheel only adds a hold gesture.

  Downsides:
  - The 3-second limit. Holding past about 3 seconds flips the knob into
    backlight mode. Fine once you're used to it, but annoying while learning.
  - It might not be possible at all. If the knob only sends its signal on
    release, holding can't be detected. That's what the test is for.
  - It's a bit fragile. Hammerspoon's key listener can occasionally stop
    responding and need a reload. Small but real upkeep.
  - Shortcuts are faster for things you do constantly. Anything you trigger 50
    times a day is better as a direct shortcut. The wheel is for the middle
    ground: frequent enough to want quick access, not frequent enough to justify
    memorizing a shortcut.

  How to decide: list the actions you'd put on the wheel. If you can name 5 or
  more you do daily (switch audio output, Spotify, screenshot, Do Not Disturb,
  lock screen, open your terminal), it's worth building. If you get stuck at
  2–3, plain shortcuts or Raycast will serve you better.

  What would you put on it?

  What I built is a Hammerspoon script: ~/.hammerspoon/knobwheel.lua, loaded by
  ~/.hammerspoon/init.lua.

  - Tap the knob: the current mode's click action (mute in Volume mode).
  - Hold about 0.35s: the wheel opens. Then either:
    - keep holding, turn, and let go to pick, or
    - let go, turn, then tap to pick.
    - Esc, or 5 seconds of doing nothing, cancels.
  - Turn the knob: the current mode's action.
